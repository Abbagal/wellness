SHINE Committee

Support, Help and Intervention for Nurturing Empowerment

SupportHelpInterventionNurturingEmpowerment

Some students go through periods when they cannot cope alone: a mental-health condition, the loss of a parent or loved one, a long illness, a family crisis, or a slide into academic difficulty. Too often these students withdraw quietly, fall behind, and are noticed only when the situation has become serious. SHINE makes sure that every such student is identified early, receives coordinated care, and is given a genuine opportunity to recover and complete their degree.

How it works

How it works

1
Identify

Academic signals, plus referrals from students, mentors and advisors.

Who SHINE is for

Mental-health concerns

Depression, anxiety or other conditions affecting daily life and studies

Personal loss

Bereavement, family crises, serious illness or extended absence

Academic distress

Multiple backlogs or a sharp decline in grades

Addiction and behaviour

Substance use, digital addiction or behavioural concerns

Disengagement

Missing classes, or not registering for a semester

Referral by others

Concerns raised by friends, mentors, faculty or wardens

How SHINE helps the student

One case owner

A single person coordinates counselling, academics and advising, so the student does not have to navigate the system alone.

Care that is connected

Counselling and psychiatric care are linked to the student's academic plan.

Room to recover

An individual action plan, with academic flexibility and policy support where needed.

Confidential and followed up

Handled with discretion and reviewed until the student is back on track.

Part B · Ongoing plans Restructuring

Peer Circles with Faculty Advisors

Restructuring mentorship for effective faculty–mentor–mentee coordination

Faculty advising and student mentorship already exist at IIT Bombay, but they often run in parallel, with little day-to-day contact between the faculty member and the student mentors. The restructuring brings them into one coordinated unit: each Faculty Advisor works directly with a small team of student mentors, and each mentor anchors a peer circle of mentees. Every student is known by name, and a concern raised anywhere in the circle reaches the right person quickly.

How coordination will work

  1. Menteeshares a concern
  2. Student mentorlistens, informs the FA
  3. Faculty Advisormeets the student
  4. Counsellor / SHINEwhen more help is needed

Student mentor Mentee · Faculty Advisor: 20–30 students across three circles

Clear structure

Each Faculty Advisor is formally linked to three mentors and their circles.

Regular contact

Scheduled FA–mentor meetings and mentor–mentee check-ins every semester.

Accountability

Mentoring guidelines followed, with student feedback on advisors and mentors.
