Overview

The Student Wellness Ecosystem

One office, five units, one continuum of care

Student wellbeing is now a national priority. The Supreme Court of India (Sukdeb Saha v. State of Andhra Pradesh, 2025) and the Ministry of Education's 2026 committee report both ask institutions to move from reactive counselling to proactive, institution-wide support.

In September 2026, IIT Bombay took the initiative of creating a new post: Associate Dean, Student Wellness. This office has been created specifically for the overall wellbeing of students. It brings five units together under one roof — the Students Wellness Centre, the Flourishing Hub, Yogastha, NSS and the Student Mentorship Program — so that prevention, support and care work as one coordinated system.

SHINE Committee

Support, Help and Intervention for Nurturing Empowerment

Some students go through periods when they cannot cope alone: a mental-health condition, the loss of a parent or loved one, a long illness, a family crisis, or a slide into academic difficulty. Too often these students withdraw quietly, fall behind, and are noticed only when the situation has become serious. SHINE makes sure that every such student is identified early, receives coordinated care, and is given a genuine opportunity to recover and complete their degree.

Who SHINE is for

Mental-health concernsDepression, anxiety or other conditions affecting daily life and studies
Personal lossBereavement, family crises, serious illness or extended absence
Academic distressMultiple backlogs or a sharp decline in grades
Addiction and behaviourSubstance use, digital addiction or behavioural concerns
DisengagementMissing classes, or not registering for a semester
Referral by othersConcerns raised by friends, mentors, faculty or wardens

How it works

Stage 1IdentifyAcademic signals, plus referrals from students, mentors and advisors
Stage 2Department reviewHead, Faculty Advisor and mentors review the case close to the student
Stage 3Institute committeeCase-by-case discussion; action plan finalised; meets every month

How SHINE helps the student

One case ownerA single person coordinates counselling, academics and advising, so the student does not have to navigate the system alone
Care that is connectedCounselling and psychiatric care are linked to the student's academic plan
Room to recoverAn individual action plan, with academic flexibility and policy support where needed
Confidential and followed upHandled with discretion and reviewed until the student is back on track

Part B · Ongoing plans Restructuring

Peer Circles with Faculty Advisors

Restructuring mentorship for effective faculty–mentor–mentee coordination

Faculty advising and student mentorship already exist at IIT Bombay, but they often run in parallel, with little day-to-day contact between the faculty member and the student mentors. The restructuring brings them into one coordinated unit: each Faculty Advisor works directly with a small team of student mentors, and each mentor anchors a peer circle of mentees. Every student is known by name, and a concern raised anywhere in the circle reaches the right person quickly.

Faculty Advisor: 20–30 students across three circles · Regular FA–mentor meetings · Mentor–mentee check-ins

How coordination will work

1Menteeshares a concern
2Student mentorlistens, informs the FA
3Faculty Advisormeets the student
4Counsellor / SHINEwhen more help is needed
Clear structureEach Faculty Advisor is formally linked to three mentors and their circles
Regular contactScheduled FA–mentor meetings and mentor–mentee check-ins every semester
AccountabilityMentoring guidelines followed, with student feedback on advisors and mentors

Part C · Future targets · 1

Wellbeing Quick Response Team

One number. One trained team. Any crisis, any hour.

The Wellbeing Quick Response Team (WQRT) will be a dedicated, round-the-clock team that handles every crisis situation on campus. Instead of a response that depends on whoever happens to be reachable, every emergency will be met by the same trained, accountable team.

WQRT · on duty 24/7
2 Counsellorsone male, one female in rotation
1 Psychiatristalways available on a phone call
2 Security guardsensure safety on site
Dedicated vehicleand a dedicated cell phone number

How a crisis will be handled

1AlertAnyone calls the WQRT number
2DispatchTeam leaves in the dedicated vehicle
3On siteCounsellors support; security keeps safe
4ClinicalPsychiatrist on call; hospital if needed
5Follow-upHandover to SWC and SHINE

Calls can come from the student, a friend, a warden, campus security, a mentor or a wellbeing ambassador.

One number to rememberPublicised in every hostel, department and on the RISE IITB app
Minutes, not hoursA team ready to move at any hour, with its own vehicle
Clear rolesTrained staff, defined protocols, and every case handed over for follow-up

Part C · Future targets · 2

RISE IITB: Wellness App and Universal Screening

Resilience · Inner strength · Self-care · Emotional well-being

RISE IITB will bring every wellness service onto one platform, with one login, and make routine wellbeing screening as normal as a health check-up. Every student will be screened, and every result will be followed up by a person.

ScreeningEvery student, every semester
CounsellingBook in person or online
Courses and workshopsFlourishing Hub, self-paced
AI self-help, 24/7Hands any risk to a counsellor
CommunityPeer circles · events

Universal screening: four categories, every result followed up

At admission (all new students) and every semester (all students, validated tools).

CategoryMeaningFollow-up
GreenThriving · no concernWellbeing content, reminders and workshop invitations
YellowCheck-in · a mild changeA gentle nudge and self-check; mentor checks in within 7 days
OrangeSupport · several signalsCounsellor reaches out within 48 hours; a slot is held
RedUrgent · serious riskEmergency protocol and the WQRT, within 1 hour

Categories are seen only by SWC clinicians and the Student Wellness office. They are never shown to students as labels and never shared with faculty.

Safeguards

  • Hosted on IIT Bombay servers; owned by IIT Bombay
  • Results seen only by authorised clinicians
  • Never part of academic, placement or disciplinary records
  • AI for self-help only; people make every decision

Roll-out in six months

AlphaPilot: one hostel, one department
BetaInstitute-wide
Full releaseAll features live

Developed through an open tender

More Counsellors, Longer Hours

Help when students are free to seek it: in the evenings and at weekends

Students attend classes and labs through the day, and many reach out only once the day is over. We will increase the number of counsellors so that counselling is available until 10 pm on weekdays and as a full service on weekends, not only for walk-ins.

Part C · Future targets · 4

Counsellor Assigned for Every Hostel and Department

Support that is local, familiar and easy to reach

Each hostel and each department will have one SWC counsellor assigned as its contact person. Wardens, Heads of Department, faculty advisors, mentors and ambassadors will always know exactly whom to call, and students will see a familiar face.

What the assigned counsellor does

  • Regular, visible presence in the hostel or department
  • First call for wardens, HoDs and faculty advisors
  • Works with the wellbeing ambassadors and mentors
  • Links local concerns to SHINE and the WQRT

Why it matters

  • Familiarity lowers the barrier to seeking help
  • Early concerns are picked up close to the student
  • Clear accountability for every hostel and department
  • Better coordination across the support network

Part C · Future targets · 5

Administrative Support for Wellness

A dedicated leadership and administrative backbone for a growing mission

Student wellness at IIT Bombay now spans round-the-clock services, a new response team, a wellness app, institute-wide screening and a larger counselling team. To run this reliably, two dedicated positions must be created: a Chief Wellness Officer and a Deputy Registrar for Student Wellness.

Associate Dean, Student WellnessOverall leadership
New positionChief Wellness OfficerProfessional lead for services, quality and day-to-day coordination
New positionDeputy Registrar, Student WellnessStaffing, budgets, procurement, contracts and records
SWCFlourishing HubYogasthaNSSStudent Mentorship ProgramWQRTRISE IITB app

Existing units and new initiatives, supported by one professional and administrative backbone

Chief Wellness Officer

  • Oversees counselling, screening and crisis services
  • Runs the WQRT roster and protocols
  • Monitors service quality and student feedback
  • Coordinates SWC, FH and the hostel and department network

Deputy Registrar, Student Wellness

  • Recruitment and contracts of counsellors and staff
  • Budgets, procurement and the app tender
  • Hospital empanelment and reimbursements
  • Records, reporting and compliance
